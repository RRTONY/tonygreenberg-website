IBOGA VS IBOGAINE RESEARCH NOTES
=================================

SOURCE: Tabernanthe iboga root bark
- Contains 6% indole alkaloids total
- Principal alkaloids: ibogaine (most abundant), ibogaline, ibogamine, tabernanthine, voacangine, coronaridine, noribogaine
- Root bark contains "hundreds of alkaloids" per some sources; at least 12+ identified

IBOGA (WHOLE PLANT / ROOT BARK):
- Raw or powdered root bark consumed in Bwiti ceremonies (Gabon, Cameroon)
- Full-spectrum alkaloid profile - "entourage effect" of multiple compounds
- More intense, longer-lasting experience
- Spiritually immersive, used for initiation rites
- Unpredictable due to variable alkaloid concentrations
- Traditional context: Bwiti religion, healing ceremonies, rites of passage
- Sub-flood microdosing also practiced

IBOGAINE (ISOLATED ALKALOID - HCl):
- Single purified compound extracted from root bark
- Ibogaine hydrochloride (HCl) is the clinical form
- More controlled dosage, precise administration
- Therapeutic, focused effect
- Fewer hallucinations, more introspective
- Used in clinical addiction treatment settings
- FDA Breakthrough Therapy potential (not yet approved)

KEY PHARMACOLOGY (IBOGAINE):
- NMDA receptor antagonist (glutamate system)
- Kappa-opioid receptor agonist
- Sigma-2 receptor agonist
- Serotonin transporter inhibitor (SERT)
- Dopamine transporter inhibitor (DAT)
- Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist
- 5-HT2A partial agonist (psychedelic effects)
- Metabolized to noribogaine (12-hydroxyibogamine) - longer-acting metabolite
- Noribogaine: more potent at mu-opioid and serotonin receptors

WHOLE PLANT ADDITIONAL ALKALOIDS:
- Ibogamine: structurally similar to ibogaine, less potent
- Tabernanthine: CNS stimulant properties
- Voacangine: anti-inflammatory, analgesic
- Coronaridine: anti-addictive properties (studied independently)
- Noribogaine: naturally present in bark, also metabolite of ibogaine
- These create "entourage effect" - synergistic action

SAFETY DIFFERENCES:
- Both carry cardiac risk (QT prolongation)
- Ibogaine HCl: more predictable dosing, easier to monitor
- Whole iboga: variable alkaloid content, harder to dose precisely
- Both require cardiac screening (EKG), liver function tests
- Contraindicated with heart conditions, certain medications

CLINICAL EVIDENCE:
- Glick et al. 1997: kappa-opioid and NMDA roles in anti-addictive effects
- Sershen et al. 1995: kappa-opioid and 5-HT3 involvement
- Multiple case studies on opioid addiction interruption
- Noribogaine has longer half-life (24-48h vs 4-7h for ibogaine)
- Growing evidence for TBI, PTSD, depression applications
